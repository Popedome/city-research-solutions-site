Copperleaf, a fictional snack brand, is planning a new protein bar line. Two questions: which bar to build, and which flavors to launch it in. Asking people what they want gets "everything, cheaper." So we used two methods built for trade-offs. A choice-based conjoint shows buyers sets of bars that differ in protein, sugar, texture, protein source and price, and asks which one they would buy. Their choices reveal what each feature is worth. A TURF analysis then finds the few flavors that together reach the most buyers.

Who answered

  • Bought a protein bar in the past month, age 18 to 64
  • Quota: 310 daily users, 490 occasional users
  • Balanced by region and by where they buy: grocery, club, convenience, online
  • Quality checks: speeders and straight-liners removed, plus a repeated task to test consistency
  • Out: works in food, retail, advertising or research

How the two methods fit

  • Conjoint picks the bar: the features, and how much each one is worth against price.
  • TURF picks the flavors: which set of three or four reaches the most buyers with the least overlap.
  • Same 800 buyers answer both, so the bar and the line-up are built for the same people.
  • Estimation: hierarchical Bayes, so every buyer has their own feature values.

Each buyer's twelve choices are turned into a value, or part-worth, for every level of every feature. The spread between a feature's best and worst level shows how much that feature drives the choice. Price matters most, then protein. Protein source, the topic of the longest internal debate, matters least.

Why the most popular three are not the best three

Cookie Dough is the third most popular flavor, liked by 30% of buyers. But 92% of its fans (217 of 236) already like Chocolate Peanut Butter or Salted Caramel. Adding it reaches few new people.

Blueberry Crumble ranks fourth on its own. But 86 of its 213 fans like neither of the top two, so it opens the line to a different group of buyers.

Swapping one for the other lifts reach from 70.1% to 78.5% with the same number of flavors.

Recommendation

A 20 g crispy bar,
in four flavors

Build a crispy layered bar with 20 g of whey protein and 6 g of sugar, tested at the $2.79 level. Against the same two competitors, its share of preference is 40%, against 16% for the current bar. Launch it in Chocolate Peanut Butter, Salted Caramel, Blueberry Crumble and Mint Chocolate, which together reach 82% of buyers. Hold Cookie Dough for later: it is popular, but its fans are already covered.

Where this study could mislead

  • Share of preference is not market share. It assumes everyone knows every bar and finds it on the shelf.
  • Buyers judged a description. If the crispy bar does not taste crispy, the value disappears.
  • Two competitors is a small shelf. Real shelves hold dozens of bars, and a live simulator can add as many as the decision needs.
