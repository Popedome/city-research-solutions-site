Fernway In-Home Use TestSample · Plant-based yogurt
Vanilla oat yogurt · 3 products · 120 households · 4 weeks
● measured   ● illustrative

Fernway wants to replace its vanilla oat yogurt and has two candidate formulas. An in-home use test puts all three products in real kitchens, blind and coded, so people eat them the way they normally would. Every household first tastes all three, one per day in a rotated order (sequential monadic), then lives with one of them for four weeks. The first taste tells you what wins a spoonful. The four weeks tell you what still gets eaten.

Control 482

Current Fernway vanilla

The product on shelf today. Included so both new formulas are judged against what buyers already know, not against each other alone.

Formula A 317

Thicker set, same sugar

New culture blend and added pea protein for a thicker, spoonable texture. Sugar unchanged at 9 g per cup.

Formula B 956

Smoother, sweeter

Smoother texture and a rounder vanilla, with sugar raised from 9 g to 12 g per cup.

Who took part Measured

116 of 120 households finished all four weeks (97%). Two moved, one lost fridge space, one stopped answering.

Screener criteria

  • Primary grocery shopper, age 21 or older
  • Eats plant-based yogurt at least twice a week, past month
  • Quota: 60 current Fernway buyers, 60 buyers of other plant-based brands
  • Quota: 48 households with children under 12
  • Four regions, 30 households each, cold-chain delivery area
  • Smartphone with our study app installed (we help set it up), and room in the fridge for 8 cups
  • Out: oat, pea or coconut allergy
  • Out: works in food, advertising or market research
  • Out: any food product test in the past six months

Placement and rotation

Six tasting orders, 20 households each, so every product appears first, second and third equally often. After tasting, households are split into three cells of 40, balanced on Fernway buyers and children.

What each household does

Three tasting ballotsTwo minutes each, right after the cup. Shown on the Flavor ballot tab.
Eat as normal for four weeksNo rules about how much. How much they actually eat is one of the results.
A short phone video when something goes right or wrongUnder a minute. A child asks for more, a cup gets skipped, the texture looks off. The prompt is "show us what just happened."
Weekly check-inFive minutes on the phone: overall liking, cups eaten, how they ate it, a photo of what is left.
Final check-in, week 4Purchase intent and the just-about-right questions repeated.

Why video and not a longer diary: people do not write down the morning they skipped breakfast yogurt. They will show you the cup they pushed to the back of the fridge.

Penalty analysis · what each miss costs Measured

Each dot is one way a product misses: for example, "317 not sweet enough." Across: the share of households who said so. Up: how much lower their overall liking was than the households who said just about right. Dots in the shaded corner are worth fixing: at least 20% of people, and at least half a point of liking lost.

The readout answers the question Fernway asked: which formula to launch, and what to fix first. It puts the first-taste result next to the fourth-week result, because they point in different directions.

Recommendation

Launch 317,
after a vanilla lift

317 was second at first taste and first by week four: liking rose from 6.9 to 7.3, households ate more of it each week, and 59% would probably or definitely buy it. 956 won the first spoonful, then lost 40% of its servings by week four once the sweetness wore thin. Do not launch the first-taste winner. Fix 317's light vanilla, keep the sugar where it is, and confirm in a short follow-up test.

What 956 is still good for

956 had the best flavor liking at first taste and the most "went right" videos in week 1, many from children. Households called it dessert. That points to an occasion, not a replacement: a smaller cup positioned after dinner may be worth a separate concept test.

Where this test could mislead

  • 40 households per product is enough to see a drop this size, not enough to split by region or by children in the home.
  • Four weeks is not a year. 317 was still rising at week 4; we do not know where it levels off.
  • People knew they were in a test. Cups eaten is a better guide than stated intent, and both point the same way here.
