Corvo Rise Assembly TestSample · Standing desk
Electric standing desk · 9 steps · 2 rounds · 40 builders
● measured   ● illustrative

Corvo sells the Rise, a flat-pack electric standing desk, online. Support calls and returns cluster around setup, and reviews mention the instructions. In round 1, 20 people each built the desk from a sealed box in our facility, using only the booklet and the tools inside, while we timed and observed every step. Corvo rewrote the booklet from what we saw, and in round 2, 20 different people built it again.

Screener, each round

  • Bought a standing desk in the past year or plans to in the next twelve months, any brand
  • Quota: 10 who call themselves handy (built three or more flat-pack items in two years), 10 who do not
  • Ages 22 to 68, a mix of women and men
  • Able to lift 20 kg with help, per the booklet's safety note
  • Out: has built any Corvo desk before
  • Out: works in furniture, retail or market research
  • Out: took part in round 1 (round 2 only)

The room

Sealed box, floor matA 4 by 4 meter build area. Only the box, the booklet and the tools inside it.
Four camerasOverhead, two side angles, and one above the booklet stand to log which page is open.
Moderator in the roomPrompts people to think aloud. Helps only when asked, or at step 8, where the booklet calls for a second person to turn the desk upright.
14-point check at the endA facility technician checks every bolt, cable and setting, so mistakes that looked fine to the builder still count.

What we measure

Task success
Built correctly without help, built correctly after asking for help, or a fault found at the final check.
Time per step
From first touching that step's parts to the last action of the step.
Error
Anything that had to be undone or left the desk wrong. Minor: caught and fixed by the builder. Major: desk would not work or had to be partly taken apart. Critical: damage or a safety risk.
Stuck
No progress for more than 60 seconds, or a request for help.
Page heat
Seconds each booklet page was open, how many builders opened it, and how often they went back.
Ratings
Ease of assembly (1 to 7) and confidence it was built right, asked before the technician's check.

What the heat says

  • Page 3, the hardware list, was opened again 4.2 times per builder. People flipped back to match screws to letters. The steps never showed the screws themselves.
  • Pages 8 and 9 (frame width) held builders for 5.5 minutes. The drawing gives a width in millimeters that nobody could measure without a metric tape.
  • Page 13 (cables) held 3 minutes, mostly with the builder holding the booklet next to the control box, trying to match ports.
  • Only 3 of 20 opened page 16. The reset instruction lived there. The other 17 never saw it.

Pages people skipped

  • Page 4, tools45% opened
  • Page 1, cover and safety60% opened
  • Page 14, turn upright70% opened
  • Page 16, troubleshooting15% opened

Skipped pages are fine when nothing important is on them. Safety and the reset are important, so in the rewrite both moved to the step where they apply.

Corvo's team rewrote the booklet with us, added a sticker to the keypad and color-coded the cable plugs. Twenty new builders assembled the desk with the new version, under the same conditions and measures.

Recommendation

Ship the new booklet
and the keypad sticker

Building the desk right without help rose from 55% to 90%. Mean build time fell from 52 to 38 minutes, errors fell from 40 to 11, and no desktop was damaged. Change the booklet's time estimate to "about 40 minutes," which is honest and achievable. Step 5 is still slow, and that is a hardware question for the next model.

Still open

  • Step 5 takes 9 minutes. It is twelve screws driven by hand with the included driver. Fewer, larger screws or pre-set inserts in the desktop would cut it. That is a parts decision for the next model.
  • Two builders still asked for help, once at step 4 and once at step 7. Both were less experienced builders and both finished correctly.

Where this test could mislead

  • 20 builders per round finds the common problems. A rare one that affects 1 in 50 buyers may not show up.
  • Our build room is clean, bright and roomy. A small apartment with the box on a rug, and a child helping, is harder.
  • People try harder when watched. Real buyers may skip more pages, which makes moving key steps out of the troubleshooting page even more important.
