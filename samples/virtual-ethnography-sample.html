Velmora, a fictional dish care brand, sells dishwasher pods and a hand dish liquid and wants to know where its next product should come from. A researcher joined 12 households on a live video call during their real weeknight dinner cleanup. Each participant propped up or carried a phone and walked through the sink, the dishwasher, the cabinet under the sink and anywhere else dishes ended up. Short follow-up tasks over the next five days filled in the nights we did not see.

A week before each visit, every participant answered a short survey about how they clean up. Then we watched. The gap between the two is the reason to do ethnography at all: people describe the routine they believe they have. The largest gaps point straight at the opportunities.

Cleanup time, minutes Observed

Timed from the first plate off the table to the last pan put away or left to soak. The longest was Keisha's two loads and a sink of pans. Every household underestimated. The hand-wash pile accounts for most of the gap.

The gap that matters most: pre-rinsing

3 of 9 said they rinse before loading. 8 of 9 did, most under running hot water, plate by plate. Asked why on camera, the answer was nearly always a version of "or it won't come clean." That is a belief about the detergent, and a detergent brand can answer it.

Every time a participant showed us something they had rigged, adapted or used in an unexpected way, the researcher captured a still from the video. Workarounds are unmet needs that people have already solved badly. Each is logged with what it is, why they do it and the theme it feeds. Images are described here.

Where they were found

    Five themes, each with the households where it showed up and the opportunity it points to. Twelve homes can show what is going on and why. They cannot size it, so each opportunity ends with the test that would.

    Where to start

    The pan pile,
    then the rinse

    The hand-wash pile is where cleanup time goes and where people improvise most. A pan product built for the overnight soak people already do is the clearest new-product lead. Pre-rinsing is the clearest message lead: 8 of 9 do it because they doubt the pod. Both go into a concept test with a few hundred dish care buyers next.
