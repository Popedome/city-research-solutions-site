How the flags are set

  • Evidence. Strong: 1,000 or more survey or diary respondents in studies where the topic was a main focus. Moderate: 500 to 999. Thin: fewer than 500, or focus groups only.
  • Freshness. Current: main-topic study in 2023 or later. Aging: 2020 to 2022. Old: 2019 or earlier.
  • Field new research when evidence is thin or old. Watch when it is aging. Otherwise current.
  • Your team sets the rules. A fast-moving category may call a two-year-old study old.

What the library does

  • Answers from your studies only. No outside sources and no guessing. If nothing in the library covers a question, it says so.
  • Cites every claim to the study, year, sample size, and the page, table or transcript time.
  • Flags method changes, such as a new question wording between tracker waves.
  • Private to your company. Your studies are never shared with or used for anyone else.

What it does not do

  • It does not make new data. A topic nobody has studied stays a gap until someone studies it.
  • It does not hide disagreement. When two studies point different ways, both are shown.
  • Judgment stays with your team. The library shortens the hunt through old decks, so more time goes to the decision.
