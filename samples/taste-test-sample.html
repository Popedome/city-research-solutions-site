Northvale Taste TestSample · Kettle chips
Sea salt kettle chips · 3 recipes · 150 tasters · central location
● measured   ● illustrative

Northvale wants to cut the sodium in its best-selling sea salt kettle chip by about 30% without losing the people who buy it. The developers made two lower-sodium recipes. A central location taste test brings 150 kettle chip buyers to a focus facility, where each person tastes all three chips blind, one at a time, in a rotated order (sequential monadic). The question: can a lower-sodium chip hold its own against the one on shelf today?

Control 241

Current sea salt

170 mg sodium per 1 oz serving. Coarse sea salt tumbled on after frying. Included so both new recipes are judged against the chip buyers already know.

Recipe A 638

Less salt, same process

120 mg sodium. Same potatoes, slice and cook as today, with about 30% less of the same salt.

Recipe B 815

Less salt, finer flake

120 mg sodium. A finer salt flake applied while the chips are still hot, so more of it sits on the surface, plus a slightly longer cook.

Who took part Measured

150 of 152 people who arrived completed all three ballots. One left early and one failed the attention check.

Screener criteria

  • Ages 18 to 64, buys snacks for the household
  • Bought kettle chips in the past two weeks, eats them at least weekly
  • Quota: 75 current Northvale buyers, 75 buyers of other kettle brands
  • Quota: half under 40, half 40 and older
  • Asked not to eat, smoke or drink coffee for 30 minutes before arriving
  • Out: food allergies or a sodium-restricted diet
  • Out: works in food, advertising or market research
  • Out: any taste test in the past three months

Serving order

Six orders, 25 tasters each, so every chip is tasted first, second and third equally often. Orders are assigned by booth and session.

Kitchen and booth controls

Same portion every timeEight whole chips per cup. Broken and folded chips are removed in the kitchen.
Bag to booth in 20 minutesBags are opened in small batches and logged, because an open bag loses crunch.
Amber booth lightingRecipe B cooks slightly darker. Under amber light, color cannot cue anyone during tasting. Appearance is rated separately at the end, in white light.

The readout answers Northvale's question: can a lower-sodium chip replace the current one, and if so, which recipe and what should change first.

Recommendation

Advance 815,
with a lighter cook

815 matched the current chip on overall liking (7.0 against 7.1, a gap that is not significant) with 29% less sodium, and buyers of other kettle brands preferred it. The hot-applied fine flake kept most of the salt impact that 638 lost. Its one flagged miss is a toasted flavor that 24% found too strong. Shorten the cook, keep the salt system, and confirm against the current chip before switching.

Where this test could mislead

  • Eight chips in a booth is a small dose. Salt and toasted flavor can build over a full bag on the couch. The in-home test checks that.
  • Amber light hid color during tasting. In the bag and the bowl, the darker color shows, which is why appearance was rated on its own.
  • 150 tasters is enough to compare the three recipes in total and by brand bought, and too few to split further by age within each buyer group.
