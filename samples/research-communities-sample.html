A research community is a group of real customers or end users who agree to take part in short research activities over many months, not one survey. CRS recruits the members, verifies them, runs the activities and keeps the community fresh. The client owns the questions and the findings. CRS holds the members' personal details.

01

Recruit

Find people who match the profile, from our own databases and the places those people already are: supply houses and trade lists for professionals, recipe newsletters and stores for consumers.

02

Screen and verify

A screener checks the profile. Verification checks the person: a license or employer check for a technician, a photo task for a home baker. Duplicates and industry insiders are removed.

03

Onboard

Members accept a plain-language agreement, fill in a profile and complete a short welcome activity. The profile means later activities can be targeted without screening again.

04

Run activities

A quarterly calendar of polls, boards, diaries, live groups and placements, each sent to the members who fit it. No member is asked more than once a week.

05

Refresh

Inactive members are retired, long-tenured members rotate out, and new members are recruited to hold the profile. Members hear each quarter what their answers changed.

Professionals are the harder build. There are fewer of them, they are busy, and they have to be verified. That is also why owning the community pays back faster in B2B.

Paid as points that members redeem for gift cards or a charity donation. Professionals are thanked at a higher rate than consumers, in line with the value of their time. Nobody is paid more for a positive answer.

Privacy · what members are promised

  • A plain-language agreement says who runs the community, what is collected, how it is used and how to leave.
  • Identities stay with CRS. Names, contact details and license numbers are never passed to the client. The client sees member numbers and profile fields.
  • Photos and video are shared clip by clip, only with the member's consent for that use, with faces blurred on request.
  • The sponsor is named unless an activity has to be blind, in which case members are told afterward.
  • Concepts stay confidential. Members agree not to share materials they see.
  • Members can leave at any time and ask for their data to be deleted.

Refresh · keeping the community typical

  • Retire the inactive. Members who skip two quarters in a row are thanked and retired.
  • Rotate the long-tenured. Members rotate out after 24 months, before they start answering like insiders.
  • Cap the asks. One activity a week per member at most, and one live group a quarter.
  • Recruit to the profile. Replacements fill the quotas that thinned, so the mix does not drift toward the most eager members.

Where communities go wrong

Overuse
Asking every weekResponse falls and only the most eager members keep answering.
Tenure
Keeping members too longThey learn the brand and stop sounding like customers.
Scope
Treating it as the marketMembers are engaged users. Sizing and share questions still need fresh sample.
Silence
Never closing the loopMembers who never hear what changed stop giving their time.

Between activities, a calibrated synthetic panel can be built from the community's own answers to screen early ideas, so live members are kept for the questions that need them.
