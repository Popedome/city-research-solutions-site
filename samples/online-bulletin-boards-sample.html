Corvane, a fictional vacuum brand, is deciding what goes into its next cordless stick vacuum. 24 people who own a stick vacuum spent four days on a private online board, about 20 to 30 minutes a day, whenever it suited them. Each day opened a new set of activities: photos of real floors and storage spots, a narrated cleaning video, a markup of the new concept and short polls. A moderator read every post and followed up with probes, in the open thread or privately.

When people posted Measured

45% of posts came after 5 pm. A two-hour group on a weeknight would have missed the people who clean after the kids are in bed.

This is how a board reads from the inside. Participants answer the activity, see each other's posts in group questions, and the moderator steps in with probes: asking for an example, a photo or the reason behind a passing remark. Private questions look the same, but only the participant and moderator see them.

People rarely volunteer a habit they have stopped noticing. One question, "what happens on the days it dies early?", turned a minor complaint into the largest theme of the study.

How we probe

  • Within 12 hours, while the moment is still fresh
  • Ask for an example or a photo, never a yes or no
  • Do not lead. "Tell me more about that" beats "So you would want a second battery?"
  • Go private when a topic is personal or a participant is quieter in the group
  • Probe the group when several people hint at the same thing

On day 3 each participant saw the Glide 2 concept board privately and placed up to six markers on it: a green marker on anything they liked, a red one on anything they disliked, each with a short comment. Four people changed their answer after reading renters describe drilling into a wall they do not own. Both numbers matter: the private one is the cleaner read of first reaction, and the group one shows how the objection spreads once it is said out loud.

How to read a markup

  • Swap-in battery is the clearest win: 19 likes, 1 dislike, and it speaks to the largest theme.
  • Touch-free bin is liked, with a doubt: 3 of the 4 dislikes ask whether it jams.
  • Wall dock drew 12 dislikes, more than any other area, almost all from renters and small kitchens.
  • Smart suction is disliked for its wording. Most comments say they do not know what it means.

Every post was coded. A theme counts a participant once, however often they raised it, so the numbers count people out of 24. Quotes are lightly trimmed. Names are invented first names with a last initial.

A board produces a lot of material quickly. The deliverables are built so the product team can follow along while it runs and act on a short answer the week it ends.

What Corvane took away

Lead with the battery.
Rethink the dock.

Runtime worry shapes how 18 of 24 people clean, and the swap-in battery was the most liked area of the board. The touch-free bin earns its place if the pack shows it does not jam. The wall dock puts off more people than it wins over: make a floor stand the default. Replace "smart suction" with words that say what it does.
