The screener is the questionnaire recruiters use to decide who gets invited. It does two jobs: it keeps the wrong people out (anyone in the industry, anyone who sat in a group recently, anyone who does not buy the category) and it sorts the right people into distinct groups. Here the split is light buyers versus heavy buyers of frozen pizza, in two markets. Red actions end the call. Blue actions count toward a quota.

The discussion guide is the moderator's plan for the 90 minutes. Each section has a time budget and a purpose, the main questions are written out, and the probes under each one are follow-ups the moderator uses when an answer needs more. The guide keeps all four groups on the same ground. It is not a script to be read word for word.

1. Welcome and ground rules

Purpose: settle the room and disclose recording and observers.

Thanks for coming. I am an independent moderator. I do not work for the company whose products we will look at tonight, so you cannot hurt my feelings. There are no right answers. We are recording so I do not have to take notes, and a few people from the team are watching from the next room. Please talk to each other, not just to me.

  • One person at a time, so the recording can follow.
  • Disagreeing is useful. If you see it differently, say so.
  • Phones away until the end.
0:05 to 0:127 min

2. Warm-up

Purpose: get every voice in the room early. No pizza talk yet.

2.1First name, who is at home with you, and the last thing you cooked that you were proud of.

  • Is cooking something you enjoy, or something that has to get done?

Moderator note: listen for cooking confidence. It shapes how people talk about convenience later.

3. Category habits

Purpose: how frozen pizza actually gets chosen, and what it competes with.

3.1Walk me through the last time frozen pizza was dinner. Who decided, and why that night?

  • What else was on the table that night: delivery, takeout, cooking?
  • Was it the whole meal, or one part of it?

3.2What is in your freezer right now? Why that one?

  • How many do you usually keep on hand?
  • Is it planned for a night, or a backup?

3.3What separates a good frozen pizza from a disappointing one?

  • Crust, sauce, cheese, toppings: which one decides it?
  • Tell me about one that let you down.

3.4Where in this aisle does "premium" start?

  • What would you have to see on the box?
  • At what price do you stop and read the box before buying?
0:30 to 0:4010 min

4. Thin crust expectations

Purpose: what "thin" promises before anyone sees a concept.

4.1When a box says thin crust, what do you expect to get?

  • What does "crisp" mean to you? How do you know if it is?
  • Thin crust for whom: kids, adults, guests?

4.2Written exercise: on your sheet, write three words for the best thin crust you have had anywhere.

  • Was that at a restaurant or at home? What would frozen need to match it?

5. Concept exposure

Purpose: reactions to three positions for the line. Show one board at a time in the rotated order. Each person rates the board on their sheet (appeal 1 to 5, plus one word) before any discussion, and marks a private first choice after the third board, so first reactions are captured before anyone hears anyone else.

Concept AHearthstone Snap

Baked on a stone hearth for a crust that snaps when you cut it. Six toppings, farm-sourced. Ready in 11 minutes.

Concept BSeasonal Table

A lighter thin crust with vegetables that change four times a year. Sized for two people, so there are no leftovers. Made for a weeknight.

Concept CNight Off

Pizzeria-style thin crust with a crisping tray in the box. Everything you like about ordering out, without the wait or the delivery fee.

5.1For each board: what is the first thing you noticed?

  • What is believable here, and what is not?
  • Who is this for? Is it for you?
  • When would you serve it? What would it replace?
  • What would make you put it back on the shelf?

5.2If only one of these were in the store next week, which would you buy first? Hold up the card.

  • What would the runner-up need to change to win?

6. Packaging reactions

Purpose: pick a box direction. Three mockups on a mock freezer shelf next to the current competitive set. X: dark matte with a window. Y: kraft look with an illustration. Z: white with a full food photo.

6.1You have thirty seconds at the shelf. Which box do you pick up?

  • What did you see first?
  • Which one looks like it costs the most? Is that good or bad?
  • If there is a window, what do you want to see through it?

6.2Hand vote: which box would you be most likely to buy?

Purpose: collect observer questions. The moderator steps out briefly.

  • Observers write questions on cards. No more than three are brought back into the room.
  • The moderator rephrases them so they do not lead.

Purpose: a final word from everyone, then release.

The headline

Heavy buyers want a night off.
Light buyers want permission.

The two buyer types wanted different things from the same line, and the split held in both markets. Heavy buyers judged a premium pizza against delivery, not against the freezer case. Light buyers wanted a reason to feel good about buying frozen at all. Crispness was the promise everyone doubted.

Key findings

1

Heavy buyers are shopping against delivery, not against other frozen pizza.

Night Off took 9 of 16 heavy-buyer votes. Heavy buyers did the math out loud: a $12 pizza looked expensive next to the freezer case and cheap next to an order for the table. The "no wait" line mattered as much as the money.

"Twelve dollars is nothing next to delivery. If it really comes out like a pizzeria, I'm in."Heavy buyer, Group 2

"Friday is the night nobody wants to cook. That's the night this is for."Heavy buyer, Group 4

2

Light buyers want a reason to feel good about it.

Seasonal Table took 8 of 16 light-buyer votes and only 2 of 16 heavy. Light buyers liked "lighter" and "sized for two" more than the vegetables themselves. Several said frozen pizza felt like a fallback they were slightly embarrassed by.

"I don't buy frozen pizza much because I feel bad after. This one sounds like dinner."Light buyer, Group 3

"Two of us, one pizza, nothing left in the fridge. That's actually the selling point."Light buyer, Group 1

3

"Crisp" is a promise people have been burned on.

A soggy center came up without prompting in all four groups. The crisping tray in Night Off was the most discussed single feature of the night, and the first question about it was always whether it really works.

"Every box says crispy. Then you cut it and the middle folds like a napkin."Heavy buyer, Group 4

4

Sourcing claims only help when they are specific.

"Farm-sourced" on Hearthstone Snap read as filler in every group. When the moderator offered a specific example, a named dairy and a named tomato variety, interest rose in both light-buyer groups. Hearthstone Snap's "snap" language was well liked; its claims were not.

"Which farms? If you're going to say it, tell me who."Light buyer, Group 1

5

Dark matte with a window wins the shelf, if the window shows the crust edge.

Package X led with 15 of 32 votes, including 10 of 16 heavy buyers. People wanted the window to show the edge of the crust, not the toppings. The white full-photo box was a solid second at 11 of 32.

"Show me the edge. I can tell from the edge if it's going to be cardboard."Heavy buyer, Group 2

Market
Buyer type mattered more than cityWithin each buyer type, Columbus and Sacramento differed by no more than one vote on any concept.

Habit
The freezer backup plan6 of 16 heavy buyers described keeping one pizza "for nights that fall apart". That points to a two-pack.
Price
Two different ceilingsMost heavy buyers put the limit near $12. Most light buyers stopped near $10.

What four groups cannot tell you: how many buyers feel this way, or what share Night Off would take. That is the next study's job.

A group has a bias of its own. Once a confident voice speaks, others drift toward it. So the guide builds in a short paper ballot: each person rates the concepts alone, in quiet, before any discussion, and again at the very end. It breaks the flow of the room for a few minutes. In return it shows what people thought before the group got to them, and how far the conversation moved them.

What the ballots add

Hearthstone Snap is stronger than the room made it look. It was the top private pick before anyone spoke. It fell once the groups picked apart "farm-sourced," and the "snap" crust language was never the problem. Fix the claims and it earns a place in the quantitative test.

Night Off's gain held up in private. The exit ballot is filled in alone, so the four people who moved to it after hearing about the crisping tray were persuaded, not just agreeing with the table.
