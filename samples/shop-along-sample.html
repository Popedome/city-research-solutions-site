Voltline, a fictional sparkling energy drink, sells well in its home stores but loses shoppers at the shelf. Its scan data shows the loss without the reason. So a researcher went shopping with 24 category buyers: 18 on real trips in three grocery stores and 6 on a delivery app over screen share. We watched the path, timed every stop and noted each can picked up and put back. A short interview followed at the shelf, while the choice was still fresh. To size what we saw, we added 120 quick intercepts at the energy drink set.

How a shop-along runs

  • Meet at the door. Two warm-up questions, then the shopper leads.
  • Follow, do not steer. The researcher stays a step behind and logs each stop and its seconds.
  • Note every touch. Picked up, turned over, put back, swapped.
  • Online: the shopper shares a phone screen and thinks aloud, and we record searches and taps.
  • Shelf interview, 6 to 8 minutes, right after the choice.

Questions at the shelf

  • What did you come in for today?mission
  • Walk me through what just happened here.replay
  • You picked that one up. What were you checking?touch
  • What made you put it back?reject
  • Do you usually buy the same one?habit
  • How much does price matter in this aisle?price

The last two questions are asked on purpose. The answers are compared with what we watched on the Said vs did tab.

Each of the four losses has a different cause, and each cause has a different fix. None of them show up in scan data as anything but a lost sale.

Reading the gap

  • Loyalty is a belief about habit. Shoppers are loyal until the shelf gives them a reason to switch: a gap, a promotion, a can in the wrong place.
  • "Price does not matter" means "I do not compare prices on purpose." The tag still gets read, mostly on multipacks.
  • Nobody mentioned shelf position. Yet 71% of pick-ups came from the two middle shelves, where Voltline had no facings in Stores A and B.

The readout turns each loss into a fix that a sales team can take to a store. Every fix is tied to what we saw, and ranked by how many sales it touches.

Recommendation

Win the turn
of the can

Voltline does not have an awareness problem at the shelf. Shoppers find it and pick it up. It loses at the turn of the can and at the tag, and it is missing where quick trips happen. Put the caffeine on the front, move to eye level, get singles into the front cooler and show price per can. Then test the new set in six stores before a wider rollout.

What is already working

  • Voltline gets noticed. 74 of 120 stopped and looked at it, and 41 picked it up.
  • New flavors pull switchers. Both shoppers who switched to Voltline did so for a flavor marked "New".
  • Regulars who find it, buy it. All three regulars who found their flavor in its usual place bought it.

Test it next

  • New set in 6 stores, 6 matched controls4 weeks
  • Fixes 2, 3 and 4 at shelfno new cans needed
  • Repeat 60 intercepts in test storesput-backs, pick-ups
  • Pass mark: pick-up to purchase over 55%from 44%

Where this study could mislead

  • 24 shop-alongs show why. The 120 intercepts size the pattern; they do not project to all stores.
  • Three stores in one region. Shelf sets differ by retailer, so we would check each key account before rolling out.
  • Being watched changes shopping a little. Shoppers took longer with a researcher beside them, so we lean on the unaccompanied intercepts for time and counts.
