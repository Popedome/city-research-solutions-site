Larkspring InterviewsSample · Reusable bottles
Insulated water bottles · 180 interviews · text and voice · 5 days in field
● measured   ● illustrative

Larkspring makes insulated steel water bottles. Its own tracking shows that many owners stop carrying their bottle within a year. The team wants to know why bottles end up in the cupboard, and whether a new lid that comes apart for cleaning addresses the problem. We ran 180 one-on-one interviews, moderated by AI from a guide our senior researcher wrote, in five days of fieldwork.

Senior researcher

Designs, reviews, reports

  • Writes the guide and the probe rules
  • Pilots with 12 people and revises
  • Reads transcripts every day of fieldwork
  • Audits probes, removes failed interviews
  • Checks the theme coding and writes the readout

AI moderator

Runs each interview

  • Asks the guide questions in order
  • Follows up when an answer is vague or opens something new, up to two probes per question
  • Shows the lid concept in section 4
  • Keeps neutral wording and never suggests an answer
  • Flags interviews that look rushed or off topic

Participant

A real, screened person

  • Owns an insulated bottle
  • Chooses text or voice
  • Takes part from home, at any hour
  • About 20 minutes
  • Paid an incentive, as in any study

Stimulus

The guide is the researcher's main tool. It sets the questions and, just as important, the rules the AI moderator follows when it decides whether to probe. Click a section to open it. Target length: 19 minutes.

1Warm-upYesterday, in their own words2 min

Questions

  1. Tell me a little about your day yesterday. Where were you, and what did you drink?

Rules for the moderator

  • Do not mention bottles, brands or water.
  • If the answer is under 15 words, ask for one more detail.
2The bottles they ownWhat gets picked, and why4 min

Questions

  1. How many reusable bottles are in your home right now? Tell me about each one.
  2. Which one do you reach for most, and why that one?

Rules for the moderator

  • If they name a feature, ask for a recent moment when it mattered.
  • Ask "why" once at most. Do not ladder further.
  • Do not name any brand they have not named.
3When it stays homeThe core of the study6 min

Questions

  1. Think about the last time you left the house without your bottle. What happened?
  2. Has there been a bottle you stopped using altogether? Tell me the story.

Rules for the moderator

  • Never raise cleaning, smell or leaks first. Let them come up on their own.
  • If "leak" comes up: ask where the bottle was and what was in it.
  • If "smell" or "gross" comes up: ask which part, and how they clean it.
  • If they say "I forgot": ask what was different about that day.
4A new lid ideaConcept reaction5 min

Questions

Concept card shown on screen or read aloudA lid that comes apart into three pieces with no hidden seal, so every part can go in the top rack of the dishwasher. Fits Larkspring 20 oz and 32 oz bottles.
  1. What is your first reaction?
  2. What, if anything, would make you doubt it?
  3. Would it change how often you carry a bottle? Why or why not?

Rules for the moderator

  • Ask for doubts even when the reaction is positive.
  • Do not defend or explain the concept beyond the card.
  • If asked about cost or where to buy: "I don't have that information. What would you expect?"
5Wrap-upOne change, anything missed2 min

Questions

  1. If you could change one thing about the bottle you use most, what would it be?
  2. Is there anything we should have asked and did not?

Rules for the moderator

  • No probes on question 2. Thank them and close.

Rules for every question

  • Two follow-ups at most per question, then move on.
  • Skip the probe when the answer already has an example and a reason.
  • Neutral prompts only: "tell me more," "what happened next," "what makes you say that."
  • Never introduce a brand, feature or problem first.
  • If someone is upset or wants to stop, end politely and flag for the researcher.
  • If someone asks for a person, pause and notify the researcher.

What the probes added

  • The first answer was "I grabbed a coffee instead." Two probes later, the real reason was the lid seal.
  • The leak probe found the bottle was on its side in a bag, a detail that shaped the readout.
  • "I'd lose one" was not on the starting code list. It was added on day 2 of fieldwork and turned up in 23 interviews.

Every transcript was coded into themes by a researcher. Counts show how many people raised each theme on their own, before the moderator mentioned cleaning or leaks. With 180 interviews, the counts can be compared across groups.

Recommendation

Launch the Clean Lid,
and lead with the seal

Cleaning the lid is the most common reason lapsed owners stop carrying a bottle: it is the main reason for 36% of them, ahead of any other. 62% of lapsed owners reacted well to the Clean Lid. Their first doubt is whether a lid that comes apart still seals, because one leak is enough to retire a bottle. Show the seal before claiming easy cleaning, then size the opportunity with a survey.

The lid is where the trouble lives 86 of 180 · 48%

Cleaning complaints were almost always about the lid and its seal, rarely the bottle. People described tools: butter knives, toothpicks, brushes bought just for the lid.

AI moderation and human moderation are good at different jobs. Many projects use both: AI-moderated interviews to map themes across many people, then a few human-moderated sessions to go deeper with the most interesting ones. Every participant in either method is a real, screened person.

What stays human in every AI-moderated study

  • The guide. A senior researcher writes the questions, the order and the probe rules.
  • The pilot. Every pilot transcript is read before full launch.
  • Quality review. Interviews that fail checks are removed by a person.
  • The probe audit. A sample of probes is read for leading wording.
  • The readout. Themes are checked and the recommendation is written by a researcher.
