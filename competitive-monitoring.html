Competitive monitoring

Three Ways to Watch a Competitor

Social listening, a shelf sweep and a purchase diary answer different questions at different speeds. Here is how they fit on one dashboard, with a sample you can click through.

Companies watch competitors three ways, and they mix them up constantly. In today's AI-forward research economy, all three are faster and cheaper to run than they were even two years ago. The tools are no longer the hard part. Knowing which one answers which question is.

Social listening: the early signal

Social listening is software that reads what people post publicly about a category, such as reviews, social posts, videos and forums, and counts what is being said. It is the earliest signal and the least precise. It tells you something is happening weeks before anything else does, but not whether it is a real shift or three loud people. The newest platforms also read images, so a product in the background of a customer's photo gets counted even when nobody names the brand.

A competitive sweep: what actually changed

A sweep confirms it in days. Someone walks the shelf, in person and online, and records every tracked model at every tracked retailer: price, list price, promotion, stock and claims. Measured, not modeled. It tells you what the competitor did. It cannot tell you why it worked.

A purchase diary: why it worked

A diary settles the why. A few dozen or a few hundred people in the category record what they bought, photograph the receipt, and say what came second and why it lost. AI-moderated follow-up interviews make this far cheaper than it used to be. It is the only one of the three that asks a person.

One dashboard, every number labeled

Listening without a sweep produces panic. A sweep without a diary produces a price war. A diary alone arrives after the decision was made. Run all three at their own speeds, on one page, and label every number as measured or modeled so the room knows which is which. Where two methods disagree is usually the most useful finding on the page.
